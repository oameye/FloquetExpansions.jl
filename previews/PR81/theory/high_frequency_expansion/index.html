High-frequency expansion

When the drive frequency is large compared with the remaining dynamical scales, the nonzero Fourier harmonics can be removed order by order. The result is a time-independent effective generator and a periodic micromotion transformation. Floquet physics usually calls this the van Vleck expansion. The same reduction appears as periodic Lie perturbation, averaging, normal-form perturbation theory, or Floquet-space block diagonalization, depending on the community. These are different representations of the same near-identity elimination of fast motion, with differences arising from notation, Fourier convention, and gauge (Van Vleck, 1929; Rahav et al., 2003; Eckardt and Anisimovas, 2015; Bukov et al., 2015; Sanders et al., 2007; Murdock, 2003).

Periodic Lie transformation

Use the Fourier convention

\[\mathcal{G}(t)=\sum_m\mathcal{G}_m e^{-im\omega t}.\]

For a general linear map generator the periodic frame transformation is

\[\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}(t) =e^{-\mathcal{K}(t)}\mathcal{G}(t)e^{\mathcal{K}(t)} -e^{-\mathcal{K}(t)}\partial_t e^{\mathcal{K}(t)}.\]

Demanding $\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}$ to be time independent defines the effective generator. In the language of Rahav, Gilary, and Fishman and of Venkatraman et al., this is a periodic Lie transformation generated by a time-dependent operator. In normal-form terminology it is a near-identity transformation; in Floquet terminology $e^{\mathcal{K}(t)}$ is micromotion (Rahav et al., 2003; Venkatraman et al., 2022). The canonical Hamiltonian version is the Hori–Deprit Lie-transform method (Hori, 1966; Deprit, 1969).

A Hamiltonian transformation is unitary, $U_K=e^{-iK}$, whereas a Liouvillian transforms by an ordinary similarity map. The recursion topology is shared, but the BCH phase is therefore algebra dependent:

\[\eta_H=i, \qquad \eta_{\mathcal L}=-1.\]

This distinction is essential once commutator corrections appear. The ordinary Liouvillian commutator itself remains the map commutator; only the Lie-transform weights differ.

Expand

\[\mathcal{K}(t)=\sum_{n\ge1}\omega^{-n}\mathcal{K}^{(n)}(t), \qquad \mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{eff}} =\sum_{n\ge0}\omega^{-n}\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(n)}.\]

The expansion is formal and asymptotic. It is useful when the off-resonant Fourier sectors are separated by frequency-scale gaps; near resonance, first move to a suitable rotating frame. Resonance-adapted Floquet perturbation and Floquet Schrieffer–Wolff constructions provide alternative treatments when this separation is not available (Rodriguez-Vega et al., 2018; Wang et al., 2024).

Averaging and the homological equation

Let $\tau=\omega t$ and split any periodic quantity into its average and oscillatory parts,

\[\langle X\rangle=X_0, \qquad \operatorname{osc}X=X-\langle X\rangle.\]

After the lower-order terms have been chosen, let $\mathcal{R}^{(n)}$ denote the known residual at order $\omega^{-n}$. The next effective term and micromotion term are fixed by

\[\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(n)} =\langle\mathcal{R}^{(n)}\rangle, \qquad \partial_\tau\mathcal{K}^{(n+1)} =\operatorname{osc}\mathcal{R}^{(n)}.\]

The second equation is the homological equation. Averaging calls the first equation a solvability condition: only the average survives in the slow dynamics. Normal-form perturbation theory calls it the projection onto the non-removable part, while the oscillatory part is absorbed into the next near-identity transformation. This is the quantum/operator analogue of classical Krylov–Bogoliubov averaging [Krylov1949, Bogoliubov1961, Sanders2007], a connection made explicit in the Lie formulation of Rahav et al. (Rahav et al., 2003).

The van Vleck gauge sets the free integration constant to zero. On Fourier components, the inverse of $\partial_\tau$ on the zero-average subspace is

\[(\partial_\tau^{-1}X)_m=\frac{i}{m}X_m, \qquad m\ne0,\]

so that $\langle\mathcal{K}\rangle=0$. This is the zero-average convention used in the standard Floquet effective-frame constructions (Goldman and Dalibard, 2014; Eckardt and Anisimovas, 2015).

Leading terms

The period average is common to both algebras,

\[\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(0)}=\mathcal{G}_0, \qquad \mathcal{K}^{(1)}_m=\frac{i}{m}\mathcal{G}_m\quad(m\ne0),\]

but the first commutator correction must be written in the physical convention of the input. For a Hamiltonian,

\[H_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(1)} =\sum_{m>0}\frac{[H_{-m},H_m]}{m},\]

whereas for a Liouvillian,

\[\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(1)} =i\sum_{m>0}\frac{[\mathcal{L}_{-m},\mathcal{L}_m]}{m}.\]

The extra $i$ is the Liouville-space consequence of the similarity-transform convention, or equivalently of the $i\mathcal L_m$ blocks in the energy-like Sambe operator.

These commutator corrections are related to the average-Hamiltonian expansion developed for quantum Liouville dynamics and extended to higher orders by Buishvili and collaborators (Buishvili and Menabde, 1979; Buishvili et al., 1981). The van Vleck recursion is related to, but distinct in bookkeeping from, Floquet–Magnus expansions (Blanes et al., 2009; Casas et al., 2001) and Brillouin–Wigner high-frequency expansions (Mikami et al., 2016).

Sambe-space block diagonalization

The same calculation can be performed on the quasienergy operator. For a Hamiltonian, write

\[Q=Q_0+Q_{\mathrm{off}}, \qquad (Q_0)_{mn}=(H_0-m\omega_d)\delta_{mn},\]

where $Q_{\mathrm{off}}$ contains the nonzero harmonics. Seek a block-off-diagonal generator $S$ such that

\[Q_{\mathrm{eff}}=e^{iS}Qe^{-iS}.\]

The first BCH equation is

\[Q_{\mathrm{off}}+i[S^{(1)},Q_0]=0.\]

At leading order in the Fourier-sector spacing,

\[S^{(1)}_{mn}=\frac{iH_{m-n}}{(m-n)\omega_d}, \qquad m\ne n.\]

The block-diagonal part of the next BCH order gives

\[H_{\mathrm{eff}} =H_0+\frac{1}{\omega_d}\sum_{m>0} \frac{[H_{-m},H_m]}{m}+\cdots,\]

the same expression obtained from the time-domain homological equation. In this representation the oscillatory harmonics are off-diagonal Sambe blocks, and the effective generator is their block-diagonal normal form (Eckardt and Anisimovas, 2015; Rahav et al., 2003; Schrieffer and Wolff, 1966).

For a Liouvillian, the Sambe blocks contain $i\mathcal{L}_m$ instead of $H_m$. Mapping the Hamiltonian-like block diagonalization back to $\mathcal L$ produces the Liouvillian Lie-transform phase above. A finite-order effective Liouvillian is algebraically well defined but is not automatically in GKLS form (Ikeda et al., 2021; Schnell et al., 2021).

Complete positivity after truncation

The loss of GKLS form at finite order is a property of the truncated effective Liouvillian, not a failure of the Floquet construction. One can instead ask for a positive finite continuation that agrees with the retained Kossakowski series through the claimed order. The active/dark Gram construction, recursive onset filtration, parameter strata, and spectral/HCM realization are developed separately in CP-preserving completion.

The recursive Lie-series construction of Venkatraman et al. is designed for symbolic calculations of this common normal form. Classical harmonic balance uses a Fourier–Galerkin projection and overlaps with averaging at a single periodic drive (Košata et al., 2022); the later quantum-harmonic-balance and diagrammatic construction of Xu et al. gives another bookkeeping language for effective-Hamiltonian calculations (Venkatraman et al., 2022; Xiao et al., 2025). These are alternative formulations of the same class of effective-dynamics problem.

Finally, formulas from different sources agree only after matching the Fourier sign, frequency or energy units, perturbative order, transformation convention, and integration constants. The zero-average van Vleck gauge is the phase-independent effective-frame convention used in the Floquet formulations of Goldman and Dalibard and Eckardt (Goldman and Dalibard, 2014; Eckardt and Anisimovas, 2015).
