# [CP-preserving completion](@id cp-preserving-completion-theory)

For a periodically driven open system, a finite-order effective Liouvillian obtained from a
high-frequency expansion need not remain inside the GKLS cone [Ikeda2021, Schnell2021](@cite).
The completion problem is to preserve every retained perturbative coefficient while choosing the
first unfixed higher-order dissipative terms so that the finite effective generator is completely
positive.

Choose a finite dissipative operator frame ``F=(F_1,\ldots,F_q)``. The retained dissipative sector
is a Hermitian Kossakowski series

```math
d^{[N]}(\epsilon)=\sum_{n=0}^{N}\epsilon^n d^{(n)},
\qquad \epsilon=\omega_d^{-1}.
```

A CP-preserving continuation is a finite Hermitian form ``\widetilde d^{[N]}`` satisfying

```math
\widetilde d^{[N]}\succeq0,
\qquad
\Pi_N\widetilde d^{[N]}=d^{[N]},
```

where ``\Pi_N`` discards powers above ``\epsilon^N``. The completion therefore changes no retained
Floquet data.

## Dissipative-frame covariance

The dissipative frame is a coordinate choice, not a set of physical decay eigenmodes. Under an
invertible frame transformation ``F\mapsto FT``, the Kossakowski form transforms by congruence.
Individual matrix entries, jump representatives, and rate assignments are frame dependent, while
positivity and inertia are congruence invariant.

This distinction permits an algebraic completion to work directly in a physically convenient
operator frame rather than first diagonalizing the leading dissipator.

## Graded Gram construction

The algebraic construction seeks a graded collapse-amplitude matrix

```math
B^{[N]}(\epsilon)=\sum_p\epsilon^p B^{(p)}
```

such that

```math
\Pi_N\!\left(B^{[N]}B^{[N]\dagger}\right)=d^{[N]}.
```

The finite positive continuation is the untruncated product

```math
\widetilde d_{\rm Gram}^{[N]}
=B^{[N]}B^{[N]\dagger}\succeq0.
```

Because only the truncated product is matched, the higher-order terms generated by the finite Gram
product are precisely the completion data left undetermined by the original asymptotic
truncation.

## Active and dark sectors

The leading Hermitian form determines an active quotient and a dark radical. In a congruence frame
adapted to this split,

```math
d=
\begin{pmatrix}
A & X\\
X^\dagger & C
\end{pmatrix},
```

where the leading part of ``A`` is nonsingular and the leading dark block vanishes. The active block
admits a graded ``LDL^\dagger`` factorization,

```math
A=L\Delta L^\dagger=GG^\dagger,
\qquad
G=L\sqrt{\Delta}.
```

This fixes one convenient jump-amplitude gauge without a Kossakowski eigendecomposition.

The active-to-dark coupling is controlled by the Feshbach/Schur complement

```math
\Sigma=C-X^\dagger A^{-1}X.
```

In an exact symbolic implementation the inverse is not required explicitly: the same information
is obtained from linear solves. Physically, ``\Sigma`` is the residual dissipative form after the
leading active channels have been eliminated.

## Recursive dark-sector onsets

Suppose the first nonzero term of the residual appears at grade ``s``,

```math
\Sigma(\epsilon)=\epsilon^s\Sigma_s+\mathcal O(\epsilon^{s+1}).
```

The Hermitian sign of ``\Sigma_s`` must be classified before its parity. A negative retained
direction is incompatible with a positive continuation on that parameter stratum. A nonnegative
even onset can be represented by an integer-power collapse amplitude beginning at grade ``s/2``;
the active/dark construction then recurses with the reduced perturbative budget.

A positive odd onset is different. The dissipative rate is regular in integer powers of
``\epsilon``, but a rate-folded collapse amplitude begins at the half-integer grade ``s/2``. Thus
an odd onset is an obstruction only to an integer-power Gram-amplitude gauge, not to complete
positivity itself.

## Parameter strata

Symbolic pivots can change rank when parameters cross special values. The completion problem must
therefore distinguish physical positivity assumptions from algebraic regularity assumptions,

```math
p\ge0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
r\ne0.
```

The first restricts the physical parameter domain. The second specifies the fixed-rank stratum on
which a particular elimination is valid. If a regularity condition fails, the correct continuation
is obtained by resolving the lower-rank stratum rather than analytically continuing a singular
pivot formula through the rank change.

## Spectral/HCM construction

A complementary construction follows perturbative decay-rate branches in a frame where the
leading Kossakowski form is diagonal. This is the completely-positive perturbative construction of
Haddadfarshi, Cui, and Mintert [Haddadfarshi2015](@cite).

For one retained branch with first nonzero rate order ``n_a``, write

```math
\lambda_a^{[N]}(\epsilon)
=\epsilon^{n_a}\left(\mu_{a,0}+\mu_{a,1}\epsilon+\cdots\right).
```

The HCM continuation determines a truncated root series and retains its finite square,

```math
\widetilde\lambda_a(\epsilon)
=\epsilon^{n_a}
\left(\nu_{a,0}+\nu_{a,1}\epsilon+\cdots\right)^2,
```

with the ``\nu_{a,j}`` chosen so that the expansion of ``\widetilde\lambda_a`` agrees with the
retained rate through order ``N``. Reconstructing the normalized perturbative branch vectors gives

```math
\widetilde d_{\rm HCM}
=\sum_a\widetilde\lambda_a\,\phi_a\phi_a^\dagger.
```

For an odd ``n_a``, the rate remains an ordinary integer-power expression although a rate-folded
collapse amplitude is Puiseux. With ``\epsilon=\omega_d^{-1}``, a positive odd-onset rate also uses
the physical orientation ``\omega_d>0``.

The spectral construction makes individual rate branches explicit but requires a suitable leading
spectral frame. Mixing within an unresolved degenerate leading sector must be treated by a separate
degenerate perturbation problem rather than by nondegenerate branch recursion.

## Nonuniqueness of the finite continuation

Gram and spectral/HCM completions can reproduce exactly the same retained Kossakowski coefficients
and still differ at the first omitted order. This is expected: the high-frequency expansion fixes
only ``\Pi_N\widetilde d``. Principal-root and SVD-based factorizations provide further gauges once
an appropriate coefficient field or numerical specialization is chosen.

The scientifically invariant statement is therefore perturbative agreement through the retained
order together with positivity of the chosen finite continuation, not uniqueness of its jump or
rate representation.

## Micromotion and perturbative order

If

```math
\mathcal L_{\rm CP}^{[N]}-\mathcal L_{\rm eff}^{[N]}
=\mathcal O(\omega_d^{-N-1}),
```

then the retained micromotion already gives the completed approximation through order ``N``. No
retained kick coefficient needs to be recomputed. A change of kick at higher order belongs to the
same unfixed perturbative data as the dissipative continuation itself.

examples.
